Every window, clubs spend enormous money on players who never fit. The tell is almost always there on day one.

## 1. The role isn't vacant

If the club already has two players in that exact position, and neither is leaving, the signing is a statement rather than a solution. Statements don't play well.

## 2. The profile doesn't match the coach

A possession coach signing a pure transition winger. A back-three system signing an orthodox full-back. Style mismatch beats talent almost every time in year one.

## 3. The price is a "market" price, not a valuation

When a fee tracks the selling club's need rather than the player's output, the buying club has lost the negotiation before it started.

## 4. The stats came from one exceptional season

One outlier year in a weaker league, in a system built entirely around the player, is the single most reliable predictor of a flop.

## 5. Nobody at the club will name who wanted him

The healthiest recruitment departments can tell you exactly who scouted a player and why. When the answer is vague, the signing was political.

---

None of these are guarantees. Players adapt, coaches change, systems bend. But when three or more of these land at once, start counting the months.
